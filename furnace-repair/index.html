From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower moving heat through the ducts. Most of the diagnosis comes down to finding the broken link. In Texas City homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Rather than guess, Texas City HVAC tests the sequence stage by stage. We read igniter resistance, measure flame signal in microamps, check that the pressure switch responds to real inducer draft, confirm voltage at the gas valve, and test the blower and its capacitor. Because this is a fuel-burning appliance, the safety basics come with every visit: we inspect the heat exchanger, leak-test the gas connections, and verify flue draft. You cannot tell a furnace problem from a furnace hazard by looking at the thermostat. Before we leave, temperature rise is measured against the data plate so the fire and the airflow are balanced as the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are stocked on every truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. Signs You Need Furnace Repair in Texas City

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue
  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • Squeals, scrapes, or grinding noises behind the blower door
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light
  • The igniter glows and clicks, yet no flame ever appears
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint

Why Professional Furnace Repair Matters

The failure that matters most in a furnace is usually hidden. A cracked or corroded heat exchanger can let combustion gases into the airstream, and nothing about it is visible from the outside of the cabinet. Delayed ignition makes things worse, because each boom at light-off puts more stress on that metal. Add high voltage, a gas valve, and a flue to the same box, and the margin for error shrinks. Diagnosis is full of traps too. A high limit that keeps tripping could be a bad switch, or it could be doing exactly its job because a filter is clogged or the blower is dying, and swapping out a working switch only hides the real fault.

Our techs look at every symptom in the context of the whole furnace. We measure flame signal instead of assuming it, which keeps us from replacing a healthy sensor while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve goes unnoticed. Draft is confirmed at the pressure switch and at the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, catching airflow problems before they shorten the life of the heat exchanger. The gas side is leak-tested with instruments, never with a quick sniff. The result is a furnace proven safe over a full cycle, plus an honest opinion if the unit is getting close to the point where replacing it beats repairing it again.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Replace the filter every month while the furnace is working hard, since it costs less than any other part
  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • Avoid packing the utility room, because burners need open space for combustion air
  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
